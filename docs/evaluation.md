## 2. MDP: backtest of the go / wait / skip policy

Every hour of the 14-day test period is replayed as "now" with the defaults (deadline 8 h, waiting cost 0.3, reading trust 0.8). The belief comes from filtering the previous 24 readings with matrices trained on the 78 training days. The realised reward uses the band that actually came: going at hour t earns `R_go(band_t) - wait_cost * t`, skipping at hour t earns `-wait_cost * t`. Past forecasts were not archived, so the MDP's forecast input is replaced in two ways that bracket the real app:

- **persistence forecast (no peeking):** every future hour is assumed to keep the band of the start hour. The policy sees nothing of the future it is scored on.
- **perfect forecast (upper bound):** the MDP is given the bands that really came next. This is the same future the reward is scored on, so the policy is close to the oracle by construction.

The oracle knows the future and picks the best hour (or skips when that is allowed).

## 4. Limits and possible improvements

- **Ground truth.** The hidden class is never observed: the HMM is trained and scored on CAMS model bands. Calibrating the reading trust `r` against street-level sensors (where public ones exist) would replace an assumption with an estimate.
- **Forecast archive.** The MDP backtest feeds past model values in as the forecast. Archiving the real 3-day forecasts each day would give an honest backtest of decisions made with forecast error.
- **Richer state.** Persistence is hard to beat with first-order transitions on a slowly changing series; adding wind speed or the trend of the last hours to the state could help multi-hour predictions.
- **QMDP.** The action rule assumes the class becomes known after one step; a small POMDP solved over a discretised belief would value waiting for information properly.
- **Exposure index.** Kinds come from OSM road classes and a 25 m rule; traffic counts or measured NO2 along streets would make the lower-exposure route evidence-based rather than a proxy.
